What we’re hearing from you is that when estimates of capacity from demand data are needed, currently the following questions are popping up.

TRUST

Fundamentally there is a problem with Trust You need the capacity conversions to ‘show their working’ so you can trust that its correct and appropriate.

But Trust is hard to come by when across the piste capacity conversion is

VARIABLE - some are simple worksheets, some more sophisticated approaches but they are fundamentally not comparable

BLACK BOX - The underlying decision making, the judgement calls, and the assumptions that have gone into the approach are not available for scrutiny.

NON-AUDITABLE - Cannot audit their approach fully: were their methods sound? Do they work for all cases you need to put them to? Can you fully understand its limitations?Are their assumptions justified? - This potentially leaves the method open to manipulation/error

ONE-OFF - often the approach is not reusable, so the money spent on its procurement cannot benefit future applications.

What we believe

We intend to address all of these issues with our OpenPlan Hospital Capacity Model

We believe in working transparently Our approach is to create something that is transparent; in our choices in the methods in the limitations This is why we code IN THE OPEN - anyone can dig into the machine and question our choices.

We actively seek input from experts - to VALIDATE that our approach is correct - applicable to the widest user group

We believe that the hard work should be on us. You have enough to handle in your own roles, understanding our methods deeply shouldn’t be needed for you to trust them. We try to maxise our tools’ impact through friction-free use, by which I mean we go to lengths to build tools that - can be understood by a motivated layman - prevent accidental misuse - are supported by accessible content that helps you interpret your results so you’re not left guessing about what to do next We want to help you to ‘show your working’ to those who would require it, by us ‘showing our working’ to you.

We believe in being honest about uncertainty. We respect that predicting the future is hard and is subject to uncertainty, that’s a fact of life. We don’t wish that uncertainty away, we don’t pretend its not there, we don’t deal in spurious certainty. We acknowledge it, bake it in to our methods and make sure that what we produce are forecasts that include that uncertainty. That way we respect your local intelligence that can be brought to bear to make realistic and mature choices based on what we forecast.

We believe that you shouldn’t need to be a statistician to have trust in our statistical approaches. We do that by repeatedly, openly, validating and verifying our work with relevant experts, recording their findings and acting on anything that requires change.

We belive in maximising value for money Taxpayer’s funds must be respected, and if we can build tools that are reusable, all the better. Our approach is therefore to build in such a way that - allows updates and extensions easily (it won’t break when new data are released) - makes succession planning easy - you don’t need to rely on that one guy in the office who ‘knows how to to the thing’, everything someone new needs to know about our tools should be simple to pick up. No more costly loss of institutional memory if someone in your team moves on.

OpenPlan Hospital Capacity Model

All those beliefs I talked about there were how we approached building the NHP demand model. That model was awarded the 2025 Florence Nightingale Award for Excellence in Health and Care Analytics by the Royal Statistical Society and the Health Foundation.

In that award, they cited its strengths as being: - “nationally consistent” - “open-source” (fully transparent) - “statistical robust” - “impact, especially in driving improvements for patients.” - “published a wealth of information and resources in easily accessible forms” - “reflects real-world uncertainty” - “it promotes transparency, trust, and alignment with local transformation plans” It was called an “unusually strong piece of applied NHS analytics”.

We plan to leverage all of that benefit with our capacity conversion model.

Lets briefly touch on how those elements are being enacted with the model we’re building you.

Transparent - Our codebases are open code by default, meaning anyone can come and inspect any element of it, and see the decision making and discussions that have gone into a lot of it. - We also host up to date open Quality Assurance logs and plans (in line with the government digital standards and National Audit Office approaches) - All assumptions will be published alongside their justifications - All limitations are clearly stated and explained - plus we provide a roadmap of future work that aims to address them where possible.

Robust We involved from the start a multi-disciplinary team of experts to guide us towards an agreed methodology.
The whole thing is openly version controlled, which means that when things change, we can always turn back the clock to find out exactly when/what/who and why of every change.

Validated We are validating the work as we go. This involved a panel including -clinical colleagues, data engineers, analysts, methodology experts, statisticians, user experience experts, and, crucially, real users.

We Account for uncertainty by sitting the capacity conversion on top of the demand model - it therefore inherits all the uncertainty considerations that are pivotal to that model. We also have plans to incorporate futher sources of uncertainty as part of our future work plans.

Adaptible We have created the tool in a way that makes future developments smooth and simple, ensuring we can keep up with updated data flows and new sources of robust information. This means the tool has longevity, should be of genuine utility to a broad anad changing audience, and reflects value for money.

Accessible We are designing the user elements with real people in mind - there should be no need to have the skills of our MDT, we want the tool to be unintimidating, useful and fool proof so the results you get are used to best effect. Crucially we’re avoiding guesswork about how users want to receive the tool - we are asking them, iteratively, to ensure we stay on the right lines and end up with something truly impactful

We aim to deliver value on day 1, and to continue to increase that value over time.

Future plans

  • Queueing theory
  • New data feeds
  • A results dashboard
  • Replacing assumptions with local operational parameters
  • Evaluation of SoA alignment
  • Baseline conversion

Our aims with version 1 of the model are to release a prototype that will deliver value from day 1. We are constantly prioritising potential extensions to ensure the roadmap is taking us towards a tool that makes everyone’s lives easier.

  • Queueing theory to better account for unpredictable demand
  • Incorporating new data feeds to remove some of the assumptions: criticial care, diagnostic imaging.
  • A results dashboard to deliver useful visualisations of output
  • Allowing local operational parameters to be ingested instead of relying on central assumptions
  • Appraisal of the elements of SoA that are not yet covered to identify opportunities to incorporate those.
  • comparison of baseline year capacity estimates to known physical capacity

As well as those extensions, what comes with the model includes:

  • seamless integration with the demand model - all improvements and updates there will allow improvement in the capacity model results
